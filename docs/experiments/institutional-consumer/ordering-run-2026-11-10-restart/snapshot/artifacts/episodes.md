# Bounded episode narratives

Read the named records with their linked qualifications. Bracketed IDs below
resolve in `../provenance/context.json`; external sources are citation-only.

## Event-stream, September–December 2018

[es-transition] places release authority at the interface between maintainers,
the registry, downstream builders, and wallet users. [npm2018] describes an
incoming maintainer's dependency change and social engineering; it does not
supply a formal succession charter. The institutional stressor is therefore
recorded as an attributed account of authority misuse, not a measured claim
that volunteer fatigue caused the incident.

[es-response] records containment and downstream response. [es-rule] separately
records BitPay's stated update rule and vendor-reported implementation. No build
inspection was performed for this pack. [es-assess-containment] asks about the
registry distribution boundary; [es-assess-users] asks about exposed users.
The latter group could bear remediation effort even after the former boundary
changed, but this pack does not quantify that burden. The two assessments are
alternative lenses drafted by the same author, not independent judgments.

A delegation-centered explanation and a downstream-dependency-centered
explanation need not be mutually exclusive. Neither is causally identified:
there is no comparison group or intervention counterfactual. [es-limit] preserves
unknowns; [search-scope] records only a bounded check for long-term follow-up.

## Ua-parser-js, October 22, 2021

[ua-transition] distinguishes publishing/deprecation capability from the ability
to remove a registry release. In [maintainer2021], the owner reports suspected
account hijack, attempted removal, support contact, and warnings. The suggestion
that policy blocked removal is tentative. [ua-dispute] preserves a possible
account/support-state explanation as an AI-authored alternative, not a second
source's testimony. The historical policy itself has not been retrieved.

The relevant resources include publication access and downstream build systems.
Maintainers and registry support face containment work; downstream operators
face possible endpoint and secret remediation. These are analytically distinct
burdens, not measured distributions. [ua-response] records the advisory's
patched-version context and its limits for judging endpoint recovery. The
advisory was updated in 2026, so its present wording cannot establish precisely
what readers knew at every point in October 2021. [ua-limit] prevents that
chronological compression.

The evidence permits bounded descriptions of authority and response. It does
not establish institutional failure rates, optimal governance, a common attacker,
or a universal mechanism shared by the two cases.

## Separate synthetic correction exercise

[candidate-synthetic] deliberately overclaims that a patched package proves
endpoint recovery. [correction-synthetic] preserves the original, replacement,
rationale, author type, and review status; [corrected-synthetic] supplies the
qualified replacement. These reuse candidate/correction reference conventions
without claiming a human correction event actually occurred. They are not part
of the historical evidence count.
