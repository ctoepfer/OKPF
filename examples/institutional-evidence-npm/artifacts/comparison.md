# Worked comparison: refuse a succession-only transfer

**Question:** would vetting a successor address both observed release-authority
incidents? This comparison is also a retrievable record, `comparison`.

**Similarities:** npm release authority, downstream dependency exposure, and
separate maintainer/registry/consumer response boundaries. `es-transition` and
`ua-transition` support this limited framing.

**Material differences:** an account of delegated maintainership is not an
account of suspected hijacking. A targeted downstream build is also not
interchangeable with an advisory's precautionary endpoint-compromise scope.
Dates, exposed groups, and observation coverage differ.

**Conjectured mechanism:** misused release authority can propagate exposure
through dependencies. This is a hypothesis for asking questions, not a causal
estimate. Supporting evidence is linked through `es-transition` and
`ua-transition`. Contrary evidence to an identical-mechanism claim is retained
in `ua-limit` (access mechanism unknown) and `ua-response` (package replacement
is not endpoint recovery). Neither source establishes a succession event in the
ua-parser episode.

**Missing information:** private handoff records, actual access mechanism,
intervention counterfactuals, historical policy, and comparable downstream
recovery measurements. The purposive selection offers no denominator.

**Decision:** reject transfer of a succession-only remedy to the ua-parser case.
Reject an analogy whenever it presumes delegation without evidence, equates
replacement availability with cleanup, or needs comparable recovery measurements
that the pack lacks. Retain only the narrower inquiry about granting, abusing,
and recovering release authority. No countermeasure effectiveness is inferred.

References resolve through `../provenance/context.json`; source-specific caveats
remain attached in both exported rows and the records. The author of this
comparison is the same AI assistant that drafted the cases, not an independent
review panel.
